* Narration:

Group Discussion: Effective Vision Statements

Effective vision statements are:
Aspirational – It should inspire and set a high-level goal for the future. 
Clear & Concise – It should be easy to understand and remember.
Future-Oriented – It should describe the long-term impact the organization aims to create.
Unique & Authentic – It should reflect the company's core values and differentiate it from competitors. 
Inclusive & Broad Enough – It should be flexible to allow for growth and innovation.
Emotionally Compelling – It should connect with people on a deeper level. 
Realistic & Achievable – While ambitious, it should still be grounded in what’s possible.
Aligned with the Organization – Your team does not exist in a vacuum. Your vision should support the mission and vision of the overall organization


What’s the difference between mission and vision?

Mission defines the purpose of an organization, outlining what it does, who it serves, and how it operates in the present. It is action-oriented and focuses on immediate goals. Example: “To heal, to teach, to discover and to advance the health of the communities we serve.“ 
Vision describes the future aspiration of an organization, painting a picture of what it hopes to achieve in the long term. It is inspirational and forward-looking. Example: “To be a premier academic medical center that transforms health and enriches lives.“ 
Mission is about the present (what we do now), while the vision is about the future (what we aim to become).

- How do we change or improve people’s lives?
- What would the healthcare learning landscape look like if we achieved our ultimate  goal?​
- What words or phrases describe what we stand for?

* Narration:

Brainstorming Activity: What is our vision?

To create a meaningful vision, let’s first reflect on why we exist as a team.

Tell participants you will ask some key questions. The questions should be posted in the room—either on flip chart paper for those in the room and written on a whiteboard, for the remote attendees. Introduce the questions and then give participants time to write down their responses on sticky notes—one idea per note. Remind them to be bold and be creative! This is a brainstorming activity!
​
Questions to ask the team:​
As a function/team, what problem are we solving?​ For whom?
How do we change or improve people’s lives?​
What would the healthcare learning landscape look like if we achieved our ultimate goal?​
What words or phrases describe what we stand for?​
​
Give everyone 5-7 minutes to write down their thoughts individually. Group similar ideas together and discuss.​
​
Now that we have a collection of ideas, let’s look for patterns. What words or themes stand out? What do we keep coming back to?
​
Highlight the themes by underlining them, moving the sticky notes, or writing them on a flip chart or whiteboard.


** Slide 5

Crafting our Vision Statement

Activity Overview:
Part 1: Indivisually create 3-5 vision statements 5 mins
Part 2: In your group, select the top 3 to present 10 mins
Part 3: Vote on the top 3 overall 5 mins

Vision Statement Prompts:
“We envision a world where…”
“Our goal is to create…”
“[Team Name] strives to…”
“Redefining the way [industry] works by…”

* Narration:

Group Activity: Crafting our Vision Statement

Now, let’s turn these themes into vision statements. We’ll do this by engaging in a 3-part activity…

Part 1

For part 1, you will work individually to draft vision statements. You can use these prompts to help you..

“We envision a world where…”
“Our goal is to create…”
“[Team Name] strives to…”
“Redefining the way [industry] works by…”

Remind participants of the discussion about what makes an effective vision statement.

We are purposefully challenging each of you to go with your first thoughts/gut instincts. This activity will be time-limited, so don't aim for perfection; just get your ideas out there. Your goal is to come up with 3-5 vision statements in 5 minutes. Write each draft vision statement on a post-it note.

Time 5 minutes and inform participants when time is up.

Part 2

In part 2 you will work in groups. You'll bring to your group the draft vision statements you created. You’ll discuss them with your group and determine as a group which are your top 3 ideas. Feel free to combine or rewrite statements to come up with 3 that the group is happy with. You will have 10 minutes in your group.

Divide participants into small groups and time 10 minutes.

Ask for a spokesperson from each group to bring the group’s top 3 ideas to the front and present them to the group.
​
Questions to discuss:
Which one is most inspiring?​
Which one feels authentic to our organization?​
Which one is clear and easy to remember?​
​
Part 3

Now we’re going to use voting to narrow down our ideas to the top 3 overall.

Provide each participant with 3 dot stickers and ask them to vote for their favorite idea(s) by placing the stickers on the corresponding post-it(s). Participants can use all 3 votes on one idea or distribute their votes any way they like.

Note: If you are transitioning to a break, you can ask participants to volte on their way to/from the break or during the break and wrap up the activity after the break.

Wrap-up

Find the 3 statements that received the most votes and read them to the team. Explain the next steps, which may vary from team to team and may include
Refining or simplify the wording of the top 3 choices
Getting input from executive leaders or other stakeholders not currently present
Vetting the options to ensure they align with the organization’s long-term mission and vision
Ultimately selecting and implementing one of the options

Note: a break of approximately 15 minutes is recommended either immediately before or immediately after the wrap-up.
