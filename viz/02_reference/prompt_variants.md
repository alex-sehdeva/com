## The four dials

Every variant is a setting of:

1. **Who speaks** — you / a neighbor who's never been inside / a newcomer / a grown-up RE kid.
2. **Time jump** — 2031 / 2036 / "when today's children are grown."
3. **What's elicited** — a description / a reputation / a change / a possibility.
4. **Register** — sentence vs. image-or-phrase.

## The variants

| # | Prompt | Samples well | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A0** (current) | "It's 2031. How would you describe this congregation to a friend?" | Warm, simple, low-barrier | Nothing forces the *future* — expect present-tense descriptions ("welcoming, friendly"), the trap the current vision fell into |
| **A1** neighbor | "It's 2031. A neighbor who has never set foot inside CUUC is telling a friend about this congregation. What do they say?" | Outward reputation — the theme SPARC Q5 shows COM cares most about and inward prompts under-sample | Misses interior spiritual life |
| **A2** hope realized | "It's 2031 and CUUC has become the congregation you hoped for. What's happening here that isn't happening today?" | Strongest future-forcing device ("isn't happening today") | Can read as criticism of the present |
| **A3** return visitor | "You've been away five years. You walk back into CUUC in 2031 and it has flourished. What do you notice first?" | Sensory, concrete — the guided imagery in one line | Tends inward/physical |
| **A4** mad-lib | "In 2031, people around White Plains call us 'the ______ congregation.' Fill in the blank, then add a sentence about why." | Most draftable output per post-it; works for a 10-year-old and an 80-year-old; fits the post-it → theme → dot-vote flow | Thin without the "why" sentence |
| **A5** Palo Alto | "What are we called to be and do?" | Theologically serious, timeless | On 100 post-its: mush. Better as the sermon frame *around* the exercise than as the prompt |

## Recommendation

If it's truly one prompt, a fusion of A1 + A2, kept to one breath:

> **"It's 2031, and CUUC has become what you hoped. What are we known for — and what would a neighbor say about us?"**

Future-forcing ("has become"), samples inward hope *and* outward reputation, produces nouns and scenes rather than adjectives. **A4 is the close second** — arguably the better fit for the dot-voting structure.

## How to decide at the dinner

Don't pick on paper — **A/B it.** Six people, two finalists, one short post-it round each. Then apply the poet test to both stacks: which pile could a drafter actually write from?

**Judging checklist** (whichever variants get tested):

- [ ] Forces the future — cannot be answered by describing today.
- [ ] Produces concrete nouns and scenes, not adjectives.
- [ ] An 80-year-old and a 15-year-old can both answer it in three minutes.
- [ ] A poet could draft from ten of its answers.

## Note on the cut tension prompt

Dropping "what should CUUC *not* become?" as a prompt is fine — but with one question, the negative space has to come from the **harvest**. The "what did someone say that was unlike everything else?" / Dissents step is now carrying that load. Protect it on Oct 18 even if the whole-group segment gets squeezed. (SPARC Q5's "compassion *and* debate" theme says the congregation values named tension — don't design it out.)
